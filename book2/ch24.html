Small Models, Distillation & QuantisationChhote Models, Distillation aur Quantisation

Bigger isn't always better. A 3-billion-parameter model that runs on a phone, answers in 50 milliseconds and costs nothing per call can beat a giant cloud model for a narrow job. This chapter is about making models smaller, cheaper and faster: small language models, distillation (a big teacher trains a small student), quantisation (smaller numbers), and cascades that use the small model first.

Bada hamesha behtar nahi. Phone par chalne wala, 50 milliseconds mein jawab dene wala aur har call par kuch kharch na karne wala 3 arab parameter ka model sankare kaam mein vishaal cloud model ko hara sakta hai. Yeh chapter models ko chhota, sasta aur tez banaane ke baare mein hai: small language models, distillation (bada teacher chhote student ko train kare), quantisation (chhote numbers), aur cascades jo pehle chhota model use karte hain.

Teacher and studentTeacher aur student

A big model is excellent at classifying your tickets, but too expensive for 10 million a month. So you let it label 20,000 real tickets, check a sample, and fine-tune a small model on those labels. The student learns to imitate the teacher on this one task, at a fraction of the cost. This is distillation, and it's how many "small but surprisingly good" models were made.

Bada model aapke tickets classify karne mein behtareen hai, par mahine ke 1 crore ke liye bahut mehenga. Toh aap use 20,000 asli tickets label karne dete ho, sample check karte ho, aur un labels par chhota model fine-tune karte ho. Student isi ek kaam par teacher ki nakal seekhta hai, kharch ke chhote hisse mein. Ise distillation kehte hain, aur kai "chhote par hairaan karne wale achhe" models aise hi bane.

AI behaviour

Small models are narrow, and fail quietlyChhote models sankare hote hain, aur chupchaap fail karte hain

A distilled model that's 97% as good as its teacher on ticket labels may be 50% as good on anything else, and it won't tell you. Its reasoning, world knowledge and non-English ability usually drop the most. Keep it on the task it was trained for, monitor its confidence, and escalate unsure cases to a bigger model.

Ticket labels par teacher jitna 97% achha distilled model baaki kisi cheez par 50% bhi ho sakta hai, aur batayega nahi. Uski reasoning, duniya ka gyaan aur non-English kaabiliyat aksar sabse zyaada girti hai. Use usi kaam par rakho jiske liye train hua, uske confidence par nazar rakho, aur shak wale cases bade model par bhejo.

Engineer's note

Other speed tricks you'll hear aboutSpeed ke doosre tricks jo sunoge

Speculative decoding: a tiny draft model guesses several tokens and the big model checks them in one pass; same output, faster. Pruning: remove weights or layers that matter least. Mixture-of-experts: a huge model that only uses a small part of itself per token. You mostly get these from your serving engine or model choice rather than building them.

Speculative decoding: chhota draft model kai tokens guess karta hai aur bada model ek pass mein check karta hai; same output, tez. Pruning: sabse kam zaroori weights ya layers hatao. Mixture-of-experts: vishaal model jo har token par apna chhota hissa hi use kare. Yeh zyaadatar serving engine ya model choice se milte hain, khud banaate nahi.

In the real world

On-device AIOn-device AI

Phones and new "AI PCs" ship with neural processing units and built-in small models for summarising notifications, rewriting text and voice features, all offline and private. For Indian users on patchy networks, on-device models can mean an app that works in a train with no signal. Plan for both: a small local model for instant, private tasks, and a cloud model for hard ones.

Phones aur naye "AI PCs" neural processing units aur bane-banaaye chhote models ke saath aate hain, notifications ka summary, text dobara likhna aur voice features ke liye, sab offline aur private. Kamzor network wale Indian users ke liye on-device models ka matlab ho sakta hai aisi app jo bina signal ki train mein bhi chale. Dono ka plan karo: turant, private kaamon ke liye chhota local model, aur mushkil ke liye cloud model.

For coders: quantise a layer by handCoders ke liye: haath se layer quantise karocode2/ch24/quantize.py 
def quantise(W, bits, group=32):
    q_max = 2 ** (bits - 1) - 1                     # 127 for 8-bit, 7 for 4-bit
    g = W.reshape(-1, group)
    scale = np.abs(g).max(1, keepdims=True) / q_max # one scale per group of 32 weights
    q = np.clip(np.round(g / scale), -q_max, q_max).astype(np.int8)
    return q, scale

7B model in fp16 = ~14 GB; in 4-bit = ~4 GB: the difference between 'no' and 'yes' on a laptop.

8-bit is nearly free: under 1% error at a quarter of the size. This simple 4-bit scheme loses about 10% per layer; real formats such as GGUF Q4_K_M use smarter grouping and scales, which is why a well-made 4-bit model stays usable. 2-bit breaks the layer.

8-bit lagbhag muft hai: chauthaai size par 1% se kam error. Yeh saada 4-bit tareeka har layer mein lagbhag 10% khota hai; GGUF Q4_K_M jaise asli formats behtar grouping aur scales use karte hain, isiliye achha bana 4-bit model kaam ka rehta hai. 2-bit layer tod deta hai.

What we learnedHumne kya seekha

  1. Small models win on narrow, high-volume, fast or private tasks.
  2. Distillation lets a big teacher train a small student for one task.
  3. Quantisation shrinks memory 2–4× with a small quality cost.
  4. Cascades ask the small model first and escalate only when unsure.
  1. Chhote models sankare, bade-volume, tez ya private kaamon mein jeette hain.
  2. Distillation se bada teacher ek kaam ke liye chhote student ko train karta hai.
  3. Quantisation memory 2–4× chhoti karta hai, thodi quality ki keemat par.
  4. Cascades pehle chhote model se poochte hain aur shak hone par hi upar bhejte hain.
