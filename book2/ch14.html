RAG from Zero to ProductionRAG: Zero se Production

Haron doesn't know your company's leave policy, yesterday's price list or your client's contract. It was never trained on them, and they change. RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) is the fix: before Haron answers, the system fetches the most relevant passages from your documents and puts them in the context, with an instruction to answer only from them and cite them. It's the single most common pattern in company AI apps.

Haron aapki company ki leave policy, kal ki price list ya client ka contract nahi jaanta. Use inpar kabhi train nahi kiya gaya, aur yeh badalte rehte hain. RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) iska ilaaj hai: Haron ke jawab se pehle system aapke documents se sabse kaam ke hisse laata hai aur context mein rakhta hai, is instruction ke saath ki sirf unhi se jawab de aur cite kare. Company AI apps mein yeh sabse aam pattern hai.

1

An open-book examOpen-book exam

RAG turns a closed-book exam into an open-book one. Part one happens in advance: cut your documents into chunks and index them. Part two happens on every question: find the best chunks, hand them to Haron with the question, and ask for an answer with citations.

RAG closed-book exam ko open-book bana deta hai. Pehla hissa pehle se hota hai: documents ko chunks mein kaat kar index karo. Doosra hissa har sawaal par: sabse achhe chunks dhoondho, sawaal ke saath Haron ko do, aur citations ke saath jawab maango.

AI behaviour

Strict grounding vs helpfulnessSakht grounding vs madadgaari

The policy says business class is allowed "above 6 hours". Pune to London is about 10 hours, but that fact isn't in the documents, and the prompt said "use ONLY the documents", so the model said "I don't know". That's not a bug; it's the trade-off you chose. Fix it in the prompt: "use the documents for policy; you may use general knowledge for facts like flight times, and say when you do."

Policy kehti hai business class "6 ghante se zyaada" par allowed hai. Pune se London lagbhag 10 ghante hai, par yeh fact documents mein nahi, aur prompt ne kaha tha "SIRF documents use karo", isliye model ne "nahi pata" kaha. Yeh bug nahi; yeh aapka chuna hua samjhauta hai. Prompt mein theek karo: "policy ke liye documents use karo; flight time jaise facts ke liye general knowledge use kar sakte ho, aur batao kab kiya."

Engineer's note

Evaluate retrieval and answers separatelyRetrieval aur jawab alag-alag evaluate karo

When a RAG answer is wrong, first ask: was the right chunk retrieved? (recall@k). If not, fix chunking, embeddings or search. If yes, the problem is generation: is the answer faithful to the chunks, and does it answer the question? Tools like RAGAS automate these metrics; a 50-question labelled set is enough to start.

Jab RAG jawab galat ho, pehle poochho: kya sahi chunk aaya tha? (recall@k). Nahi, toh chunking, embeddings ya search theek karo. Haan, toh dikkat generation mein hai: kya jawab chunks ke prati wafaadaar hai, aur kya sawaal ka jawab deta hai? RAGAS jaise tools yeh metrics automate karte hain; shuru karne ke liye 50 sawaalon ka labelled set kaafi hai.

In the real world

Permissions are part of retrievalPermissions retrieval ka hissa hain

If the index contains salary sheets and board minutes, a clever question from an intern can pull them in. Store each chunk's access rights and filter by the asking user's permissions before ranking. Keep the index in sync when documents change or are deleted, especially personal data that someone asked you to erase.

Agar index mein salary sheets aur board minutes hain, toh intern ka chalaak sawaal unhe kheench sakta hai. Har chunk ke access rights store karo aur ranking se pehle poochne wale user ki permissions se filter karo. Documents badalne ya delete hone par index sync rakho, khaaskar woh personal data jise kisi ne mitaane ko kaha.

For coders: the complete local pipelineCoders ke liye: poori local pipelinecode2/ch14/rag_local.py 
chunks = [{"id": title, "text": f"{title}: {body}"} for title, body in sections(DOC)]   # 1 chunk by heading
vectors = embed([c["text"] for c in chunks])                                         # 2 embed once

def retrieve(q, k=2):
    vec = [cos(embed([q])[0], v) for v in vectors]                                   # 3a meaning
    kw  = [keyword_score(q, c["text"]) for c in chunks]                              # 3b exact words
    fused = sorted(range(len(chunks)), key=lambda i: -(1/(60+rank(vec)[i]) + 1/(60+rank(kw)[i])))
    return [chunks[i] for i in fused[:k]]                                            # 3c RRF

prompt = ("Answer using ONLY the documents. Cite the doc id in square brackets. "
          "If the documents don't contain the answer, say \"I don't know\".\n\n"
          + "\n".join(f'<doc id="{c["id"]}">{c["text"]}</doc>' for c in hits)
          + f"\n\nQuestion: {q}")                                                     # 4 grounded prompt

What we learnedHumne kya seekha

  1. RAG = fetch the right passages, then answer from them with citations.
  2. Index once (chunk, embed, store); retrieve on every question.
  3. Most failures are retrieval failures; measure recall first.
  4. Use hybrid search, reranking and permission filters in production.
  1. RAG = sahi hisse laao, phir unse citations ke saath jawab do.
  2. Ek baar index karo (chunk, embed, store); har sawaal par retrieve.
  3. Zyaadatar failures retrieval ke hain; pehle recall naapo.
  4. Production mein hybrid search, reranking aur permission filters use karo.
