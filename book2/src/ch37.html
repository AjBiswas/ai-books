Every model in this book, from a spam filter to a frontier LLM, learns the same way: guess, measure how wrong the guess was, nudge the numbers to be a little less wrong, repeat millions of times. This chapter opens that machine with two tiny programs you can run in seconds: one learns a straight line, the other learns a puzzle that needs a hidden layer. No calculus degree needed, just the idea of "walking downhill".

Is book ka har model, spam filter se lekar frontier LLM tak, ek hi tarah seekhta hai: andaaza lagao, naapo andaaza kitna galat tha, numbers ko thoda kam galat hone ke liye khiskao, dohraao, laakhon baar. Yeh chapter us machine ko do chhote programs se kholta hai jo seconds mein chalte hain: ek seedhi line seekhta hai, doosra ek paheli jise hidden layer chahiye. Calculus ki degree nahi chahiye, bas "dhalaan par neeche chalne" ka idea.

1

A model is a function with knobsModel knobs wala ek function hai

Imagine a radio with millions of tuning knobs. Turn them one way and it plays noise; turn them just right and it plays music. A neural network is that radio. The knobs are called weights (or parameters): Qwen3-4B has about 4 billion of them. "Training" means finding knob positions that make the outputs right. Before training, the knobs are random and the model talks nonsense, exactly like the tiny GPT in Chapter 39 before its first step.

Socho ek radio jisme laakhon tuning knobs hain. Ek taraf ghumao toh shor; bilkul sahi ghumao toh sangeet. Neural network wahi radio hai. Knobs ko weights (ya parameters) kehte hain: Qwen3-4B mein lagbhag 4 arab hain. "Training" ka matlab hai aisi knob positions dhoondhna jisse outputs sahi aayein. Training se pehle knobs random hote hain aur model bakwaas bolta hai, bilkul Chapter 39 ke tiny GPT ki tarah pehle step se pehle.

2

Gradient descent: walking downhill in fogGradient descent: kohre mein dhalaan par neeche chalna

You're on a hill in thick fog and want to reach the valley. You can't see it, but you can feel which way the ground slopes under your feet. So you take a small step downhill, feel again, step again. That's gradient descent. The "height" is the loss: one number that says how wrong the model is. The "slope" is the gradient: for each weight, which direction makes the loss smaller. The step size is the learning rate: too small and you crawl, too big and you jump over the valley.

Aap gehre kohre mein ek pahaadi par ho aur ghaati tak pahunchna hai. Ghaati dikhti nahi, par pairon ke neeche zameen kis taraf jhukti hai woh mehsoos hota hai. Toh aap neeche ki taraf chhota kadam lete ho, phir mehsoos karte ho, phir kadam. Yahi gradient descent hai. "Oonchaai" hai loss: ek number jo batata hai model kitna galat hai. "Dhalaan" hai gradient: har weight ke liye, kis disha mein loss chhota hoga. Kadam ka size hai learning rate: bahut chhota toh rengte ho, bahut bada toh ghaati ke upar se kood jaate ho.

For coders: learn y = 2x + 1 from 50 noisy pointsCoders ke liye: 50 shor wale points se y = 2x + 1 seekhocode2/ch37/learn_line.py 
w, b, lr = 0.0, 0.0, 0.05                     # start knowing nothing; lr = step size
for step in range(101):
    err = (w * x + b) - y
    loss = np.mean(err ** 2)                  # mean squared error: how wrong we are
    dw = np.mean(2 * err * x)                 # slope of the loss with respect to w
    db = np.mean(2 * err)                     # ... and with respect to b
    w -= lr * dw                              # step downhill
    b -= lr * db

3

Neurons and layersNeurons aur layers

A neuron is tiny: multiply each input by a weight, add them up, add a bias, then pass the result through a bend called an activation (such as tanh or ReLU). One neuron can only draw a straight dividing line. Stack neurons into a layer, and layers on top of layers, and the bends combine into curves that can separate almost anything. That's the whole trick of "deep" learning: many simple bends, stacked.

Neuron chhota sa hai: har input ko ek weight se guna karo, jodo, ek bias jodo, phir nateeje ko ek mod se guzaaro jise activation kehte hain (jaise tanh ya ReLU). Ek neuron sirf seedhi bantwaara line kheench sakta hai. Neurons ko layer mein jodo, aur layers ke upar layers, toh mod milkar curves bante hain jo lagbhag kuch bhi alag kar sakte hain. "Deep" learning ki poori chaal yahi hai: bahut saare saade mod, ek ke upar ek.

The classic test is XOR: output 1 when exactly one of two inputs is 1. Plot the four cases and you'll see no single straight line separates the 1s from the 0s. One hidden layer of four neurons solves it.

Classic test hai XOR: output 1 jab do inputs mein se theek ek 1 ho. Chaaron cases plot karo toh dikhega ki koi ek seedhi line 1s ko 0s se alag nahi karti. Chaar neurons ki ek hidden layer ise suljha deti hai.

4

Backpropagation: passing the blame backwardsBackpropagation: dosh ko peeche ki taraf bhejna

With one weight, finding the slope is easy. With four billion, you need a clever shortcut. Backpropagation is that shortcut. After a forward pass computes the loss, it walks backwards through the network, layer by layer, and works out how much each weight contributed to the error, reusing the work of the layer above (that's the "chain rule"). One backward pass costs about as much as two forward passes, for all the weights at once. Libraries like PyTorch do it automatically; here we write it by hand once, and then check it against a slow but simple numerical estimate.

Ek weight ho toh dhalaan nikaalna aasaan hai. Chaar arab ho toh chaalaak shortcut chahiye. Backpropagation wahi shortcut hai. Forward pass loss nikaalta hai, phir yeh network mein peeche ki taraf, layer-dar-layer chalta hai, aur hisaab lagaata hai ki har weight ne galti mein kitna hissa diya, upar wali layer ka kaam dobara use karke (yahi "chain rule" hai). Ek backward pass lagbhag do forward passes jitna kharcha karta hai, saare weights ke liye ek saath. PyTorch jaisi libraries yeh apne aap karti hain; yahan hum ek baar haath se likhte hain, phir dheemi par saadi numerical estimate se check karte hain.

For coders: a two-layer network learns XOR, with a gradient checkCoders ke liye: do-layer network XOR seekhta hai, gradient check ke saathcode2/ch37/tiny_net.py 
h = np.tanh(X @ W1 + b1)                    # forward: hidden layer of 4 neurons
p = sigmoid(h @ W2 + b2)                    # output: probability of "1"

dz2 = (p - Y) / n                           # backward: loss -> output
gW2 = h.T @ dz2
dh  = dz2 @ W2.T                            # pass the blame back to the hidden layer
dz1 = dh * (1 - h ** 2)                     # through tanh
gW1 = X.T @ dz1

AI behaviour

Overfitting: memorising instead of learningOverfitting: seekhne ki jagah ratna

A network with enough knobs can memorise its training data, noise included, and then fail on anything new, like a student who memorised last year's answer key. That's why we always keep a validation set the model never trains on, and stop when its loss stops improving. The same idea, scaled up, is why evals (Ch 26) use questions the model hasn't seen.

Kaafi knobs wala network apna training data, shor samet, rat sakta hai, aur phir kisi bhi nayi cheez par fail ho jaata hai, jaise pichhle saal ki answer key ratne wala student. Isliye hum hamesha ek validation set rakhte hain jis par model kabhi train nahi hota, aur jab uska loss sudharna band ho tab ruk jaate hain. Yahi idea bade scale par wajah hai ki evals (Ch 26) aise sawaal use karte hain jo model ne nahi dekhe.

Engineer's note

From here to a real LLMYahan se asli LLM tak

An LLM is this same loop with three changes: the input is text turned into tokens, the network is a stack of transformer blocks (Ch 38), and the loss asks "how surprised were you by the real next token?" (cross-entropy). The optimiser is usually Adam, a smarter version of "step downhill" that adapts the step size for each weight. Chapter 39 puts all of it together and trains a tiny GPT on your laptop.

LLM yahi loop hai teen badlaav ke saath: input text hai jo tokens mein badla gaya, network transformer blocks ka dher hai (Ch 38), aur loss poochhta hai "asli agle token se tum kitna chaunke?" (cross-entropy). Optimiser aksar Adam hota hai, "neeche kadam" ka smart version jo har weight ke liye kadam ka size badalta hai. Chapter 39 sab jod kar aapke laptop par ek tiny GPT train karta hai.

What we learnedHumne kya seekha

  1. A model is a function with many knobs (weights); training finds good knob settings.
  2. The loss measures how wrong the model is; the gradient says which way to turn each knob.
  3. Gradient descent takes small steps downhill; the learning rate sets the step size.
  4. Stacked layers with bends (activations) can learn curves a single neuron can't.
  5. Backpropagation computes every gradient in one backward pass; check it numerically once.
  1. Model bahut saare knobs (weights) wala function hai; training achhi knob settings dhoondhti hai.
  2. Loss naapta hai model kitna galat hai; gradient batata hai har knob kis taraf ghumaana hai.
  3. Gradient descent neeche ki taraf chhote kadam leta hai; learning rate kadam ka size tay karta hai.
  4. Mod (activations) wali layers ka dher woh curves seekh sakta hai jo ek neuron nahi.
  5. Backpropagation ek backward pass mein har gradient nikaalta hai; ek baar numerically check karo.
