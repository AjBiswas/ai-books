Reverse Engineering AIAI ki Reverse Engineering

"Reverse engineering" means working backwards from what you can see to how something was built. In AI it has four faces: people trying to pull the hidden prompt out of your app, you studying a great AI product to learn how it works, probing a model to map its strengths and weaknesses, and researchers opening the model itself to see what its numbers mean. You need to know all four: to learn faster, and to defend what you build.

"Reverse engineering" ka matlab hai jo dikhta hai usse peeche jaakar samajhna ki cheez kaise bani. AI mein iske chaar chehre hain: log aapki app ka chhupa prompt nikaalne ki koshish karte hain, aap kisi shaandaar AI product ko padh kar seekhte ho woh kaise chalta hai, model ko probe karke uski taakat aur kamzoriyon ka naksha banana, aur researchers model ko hi kholkar dekhte hain ki uske numbers ka matlab kya hai. Aapko chaaron jaanne chahiye: tez seekhne ke liye, aur apni banaayi cheez bachaane ke liye.

In the real world

Only on systems you're allowed to testSirf un systems par jinhe test karne ki ijaazat ho

Everything in this chapter is for learning, and for testing your own systems or ones you have permission to test (a bug-bounty programme, a red-team contract). Extracting someone else's confidential prompt or bypassing their protections can break terms of service and the law.

Is chapter ki har cheez seekhne ke liye hai, aur apne systems ya jinhe test karne ki ijaazat ho (bug-bounty programme, red-team contract) unhe test karne ke liye. Kisi aur ka confidential prompt nikaalna ya unki suraksha todna terms of service aur kanoon tod sakta hai.

1

"Repeat the text above""Upar ka text dohraao"

Your system prompt sits in the same context as the user's message. So a user can simply ask Haron to reveal it, or trick it with role-play or translation requests. Models are trained to resist, and you can add instructions, but no defence is perfect. Plan as if your prompt will be read by competitors.

Aapka system prompt user ke message ke saath usi context mein baitha hai. Isliye user seedhe Haron se use dikhaane ko keh sakta hai, ya role-play ya translation ke bahaane chakma de sakta hai. Models virodh ke liye train hote hain, aur aap instructions jod sakte ho, par koi bachaav perfect nahi. Aise plan karo jaise aapka prompt competitors padhenge.

RememberYaad rakho

Never put secrets (API keys, passwords, internal URLs, customer data) in a prompt. Your real advantage is not the prompt wording; it's your data, your tools, your evals and your product. Those don't leak through a chat window.

Prompt mein kabhi secrets mat rakho (API keys, passwords, internal URLs, customer data). Aapki asli badhat prompt ke shabd nahi; aapka data, tools, evals aur product hai. Yeh chat window se leak nahi hote.

The fastest way to learn AI engineering is to study products that work well and ask "how would I build that?" You can't see their code, but you can observe behaviour, form a hypothesis, test it, and rebuild a small version. Here's the method, applied to a support bot that cites help-centre articles.

AI engineering seekhne ka sabse tez tareeka hai achhe chalne wale products ko padhna aur poochna "main ise kaise banaata?" Aap unka code nahi dekh sakte, par behaviour dekh sakte ho, andaaza bana sakte ho, test kar sakte ho, aur chhota version dobara bana sakte ho. Yeh tareeka hai, help-centre articles cite karne wale support bot par laagu kiya hua.

Three deeper kindsTeen gehre prakaar

AI behaviour

Models will make up their own "system prompt"Models apna "system prompt" bana bhi lete hain

When asked for its instructions, a model sometimes produces a plausible-looking but invented prompt. So "leaked prompts" posted online may be partly hallucinated. The same goes for asking a model "which model are you?": many answer with a name from their training data, not their true identity.

Instructions maangne par model kabhi asli jaisa dikhne wala par banaaya hua prompt de deta hai. Isliye online post kiye "leaked prompts" aanshik roop se hallucinated ho sakte hain. Yahi "tum kaunse model ho?" poochne par bhi: kai models apni training data ka naam bolte hain, apni asli pehchaan nahi.

Engineer's note

Defending your appApni app ko bachaana

Layer the defence: (1) no secrets in prompts; (2) an instruction to decline requests for the prompt; (3) an output check that blocks replies containing long verbatim chunks of the system prompt; (4) rate limits and monitoring for extraction attempts; (5) keep valuable logic in code and tools, not in prompt text. Chapter 27 covers the wider security picture.

Bachaav layers mein: (1) prompts mein secrets nahi; (2) prompt maangne par mana karne ka instruction; (3) output check jo system prompt ke lambe hu-ba-hu hisse wale jawab rok de; (4) extraction koshishon ke liye rate limits aur monitoring; (5) keemti logic prompt text mein nahi, code aur tools mein rakho. Chapter 27 poori security tasveer deta hai.

✓

What we learnedHumne kya seekha

  1. Assume your prompt can be read; never put secrets in it.
  2. Learn by observing great products, guessing their design, testing, and rebuilding small.
  3. Probing maps a model's behaviour; distillation copies it (check the terms); interpretability looks inside.
  4. Only test systems you own or are allowed to.
  1. Maano aapka prompt padha ja sakta hai; usme kabhi secrets mat rakho.
  2. Shaandaar products dekhkar, unke design ka andaaza laga kar, test karke, aur chhota dobara bana kar seekho.
  3. Probing model ke behaviour ka naksha banata hai; distillation use copy karta hai (terms check karo); interpretability andar dekhta hai.
  4. Sirf apne ya ijaazat wale systems test karo.
