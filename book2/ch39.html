Build a Tiny LLM from ScratchShunya se Tiny LLM Banao

Time to build Haron's brain yourself. In this chapter you train a real GPT, small enough to understand line by line and fast enough to train in under a minute on a laptop CPU. It has every part from Chapters 37 and 38: embeddings, positions, causal self-attention, an MLP, residuals, a softmax over the vocabulary, a hand-written backward pass, and the Adam optimiser. It reads one character at a time from a little story about Haron, and learns to write in that style. It won't be clever; it will be yours, and nothing about LLMs will feel like magic again.

Ab Haron ka dimaag khud banaane ka samay hai. Is chapter mein aap ek asli GPT train karte ho, itna chhota ki line-dar-line samajh aaye aur itna tez ki laptop CPU par ek minute se kam mein train ho jaaye. Isme Chapter 37 aur 38 ka har hissa hai: embeddings, positions, causal self-attention, MLP, residuals, vocabulary par softmax, haath se likha backward pass, aur Adam optimiser. Yeh Haron ki ek chhoti kahani se ek-ek character padhta hai, aur usi andaaz mein likhna seekhta hai. Yeh chaalaak nahi hoga; yeh aapka hoga, aur iske baad LLMs kabhi jaadu nahi lagenge.

1

The data: predicting the next characterData: agla character predict karna

Our training text is haron.txt: 1,872 characters of simple sentences ("haron calls the weather tool. the tool says it is sunny."). The vocabulary is every distinct character: 26 letters, space, full stop and newline make 27 (lowercase only, to keep it tiny). Real LLMs use sub-word tokens instead (Ch 2), but the job is identical. From any position, take 32 characters as input; the target is the same 32 characters shifted one place to the right. So one window teaches 32 lessons at once: after "h" comes "a", after "ha" comes "r", after "har" comes "o", and so on.

Hamara training text hai haron.txt: aasaan vaakyon ke 1,872 characters ("haron calls the weather tool. the tool says it is sunny."). Vocabulary har alag character hai: 26 akshar, space, full stop aur newline milakar 27 (chhota rakhne ke liye sirf lowercase). Asli LLMs iski jagah sub-word tokens use karte hain (Ch 2), par kaam bilkul wahi hai. Kisi bhi jagah se 32 characters input lo; target wahi 32 characters ek jagah daayein khiske hue. Toh ek window ek saath 32 sabak sikhaati hai: "h" ke baad "a", "ha" ke baad "r", "har" ke baad "o", aur aage.

2

The model: one GPT blockModel: ek GPT block

Width 48, one attention head, one block, context of 32 characters: 32,043 weights in total. For scale, Qwen3-4B has about 125,000 times more. The forward pass is the block from Chapter 38. The backward pass is about 30 lines of numpy, each the chain rule applied to one forward line, in reverse order. Before training, we compare the hand-written gradients with numerical estimates on three different weight matrices: the largest difference is 0.00000001. That check is what lets you trust everything after it.

Width 48, ek attention head, ek block, 32 characters ka context: kul 32,043 weights. Tulna ke liye, Qwen3-4B mein lagbhag 1,25,000 guna zyaada hain. Forward pass Chapter 38 wala block hai. Backward pass numpy ki lagbhag 30 lines hai, har line ek forward line par chain rule, ulte kram mein. Training se pehle hum teen alag weight matrices par haath ke likhe gradients ko numerical estimates se milaate hain: sabse bada farak 0.00000001. Yahi check aage ki har cheez par bharosa dilaata hai.

For coders: the heart of tiny_gpt.pyCoders ke liye: tiny_gpt.py ka dilcode2/ch39/tiny_gpt.py 
def forward(P, idx, targets):
    x0 = P["E"][idx] + P["pos"][:n]                       # token + position embeddings
    q, k, v = x0 @ P["Wq"], x0 @ P["Wk"], x0 @ P["Wv"]
    sc = q @ k.transpose(0, 2, 1) / np.sqrt(d)
    sc[:, MASK[:n, :n]] = -1e9                            # causal mask
    a = softmax(sc)
    x1 = x0 + (a @ v) @ P["Wo"]                           # attention + residual
    h = np.maximum(0, x1 @ P["W1"] + P["b1"])
    x2 = x1 + h @ P["W2"] + P["b2"]                       # MLP + residual
    logits = x2 @ P["Wout"] + P["bout"]                   # a score for each of 27 characters
    loss = -mean(log(softmax(logits)[targets]))           # cross-entropy: surprise at the truth

# training: Adam, 2,000 steps, batches of 32 windows
for step in range(1, 2001):
    _, loss, cache = forward(P, *batch())
    g = backward(P, cache, targets)                       # ~30 lines of chain rule
    adam_update(P, g)

Training: watch it learnTraining: seekhte hue dekho

Loss is "how surprised was the model by the real next character", on average. A model that guesses uniformly among 27 characters scores ln(27) = 3.30, and our untrained model scores 3.29: it knows nothing. Within 500 steps it has learned spelling and common words; by 2,000 steps, phrases and sentence shapes.

Loss hai "asli agle character se model kitna chaunka", ausatan. 27 characters mein barabar andaaza lagaane wala model ln(27) = 3.30 score karta hai, aur hamara untrained model 3.29: use kuch nahi pata. 500 steps mein woh spelling aur aam shabd seekh leta hai; 2,000 steps tak, phrases aur vaakyon ka aakaar.

AI behaviour

Look closely at the mistakesGaltiyon ko dhyaan se dekho

"the weather in pune rassword" blends "pune" with "password" from another sentence. "haron writes a shop tool" glues two real phrases into one that never appeared. And it says the Mumbai train answer is "cloudy". These are hallucinations in miniature: the model produces fluent, plausible text from patterns, with no idea what is true. Big LLMs make the same kind of error, just much more rarely and much more convincingly (Book 2, Ch 3).

"the weather in pune rassword" mein "pune" aur doosre vaakya ka "password" ghul gaye. "haron writes a shop tool" do asli phrases ko jod kar aisa bana deta hai jo kabhi aaya hi nahi. Aur Mumbai train ke jawab mein "cloudy" kehta hai. Yeh chhote roop mein hallucinations hain: model patterns se dhaaraapravaah, sach jaisa text banata hai, bina jaane ki sach kya hai. Bade LLMs bhi aisi hi galti karte hain, bas bahut kam aur bahut zyaada yakeen dilaane wale tareeke se (Book 2, Ch 3).

Engineer's note

When would you actually train from scratch?Asal mein shunya se train kab karoge?

Almost never for a product: start with an existing model, then prompt, retrieve, or fine-tune (Ch 23). Training from scratch makes sense for research, for tiny specialised models (a classifier for one data type, an on-device keyboard model), or for learning. To go further after this chapter, rewrite tiny_gpt.py in PyTorch: autograd replaces the hand-written backward pass, and a GPU makes 1,000× bigger models practical.

Product ke liye lagbhag kabhi nahi: maujooda model se shuru karo, phir prompt, retrieve, ya fine-tune (Ch 23). Shunya se training research ke liye, chhote khaas models ke liye (ek data type ka classifier, on-device keyboard model), ya seekhne ke liye theek hai. Is chapter ke baad aage badhne ke liye tiny_gpt.py ko PyTorch mein dobara likho: autograd haath ke likhe backward pass ki jagah le leta hai, aur GPU 1,000× bade models sambhav bana deta hai.

What we learnedHumne kya seekha

  1. An LLM is trained to predict the next token: inputs and targets are the same text shifted by one.
  2. A one-block GPT with 32,043 weights learns words and sentence patterns in 42 seconds.
  3. Check hand-written gradients numerically before trusting them.
  4. Its mistakes are hallucinations in miniature: fluent patterns, no grasp of truth.
  5. Real LLMs are the same idea with far more data, width, depth and post-training.
  1. LLM agla token predict karne ke liye train hota hai: inputs aur targets ek jagah khiska hua wahi text.
  2. 32,043 weights wala ek-block GPT 42 second mein shabd aur vaakyon ke pattern seekh leta hai.
  3. Haath ke likhe gradients par bharosa karne se pehle numerically check karo.
  4. Iski galtiyan chhote roop mein hallucinations hain: dhaaraapravaah patterns, sach ki samajh nahi.
  5. Asli LLMs wahi idea hain, bahut zyaada data, width, depth aur post-training ke saath.
