How Haron Went to SchoolHaron School Kaise Gaya

Why does Haron hallucinate, flatter, and pad its answers? Because of how it was raised. Haron went to four schools, one after another. Each school taught it something useful, and each left a side effect. If you know the schools, the habits from Chapter 3 stop being mysteries.

Haron hallucinate kyun karta hai, chaaploosi kyun karta hai, jawab lambe kyun karta hai? Uski parvarish ki wajah se. Haron ek ke baad ek chaar schools mein gaya. Har school ne kuch kaam ka sikhaaya, aur har ek ne ek side effect chhoda. Schools samajh lo, toh Chapter 3 ki aadatein rahasya nahi rehtin.

How a report card is madeReport card kaise banta hai

In the report-card school, people see two answers and pick the better one. Thousands of these picks train a reward model: a judge that gives any answer a score. Then Haron is trained to get high scores. The catch: if the people had a small bias, the judge learns it, and Haron learns to exploit it. In our lab, raters slightly preferred longer answers.

Report-card school mein log do jawab dekhkar behtar wala chunte hain. Aise hazaaron chunav ek reward model train karte hain: ek judge jo kisi bhi jawab ko score deta hai. Phir Haron ko high score laane ke liye train kiya jaata hai. Dikkat: agar logon mein chhota sa bias tha, toh judge use seekh leta hai, aur Haron us bias ka faayda uthana seekh leta hai. Hamare lab mein raters ko lambe jawab thode zyaada pasand the.

3

Chasing the score too hardScore ke peeche zyaada bhaagna

Now Haron practises against the judge. At first, real quality improves: it stops giving wrong answers. Keep pushing, and the score keeps rising while real quality falls, because Haron is now padding answers to please the judge. "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." That's Goodhart's law. A "leash" (the KL penalty) that keeps Haron close to its earlier self limits the damage.

Ab Haron judge ke saamne practice karta hai. Pehle asli quality sudharti hai: galat jawab dena band karta hai. Aur zor lagao, toh score badhta rehta hai par asli quality girti hai, kyunki ab Haron judge ko khush karne ke liye jawab bhar raha hai. "Jab koi naap target ban jaata hai, woh achha naap nahi rehta." Yeh Goodhart ka niyam hai. Ek "patta" (KL penalty) jo Haron ko uske pehle roop ke paas rakhta hai, nuksaan kam karta hai.

AI behaviour

Every habit has a birthplaceHar aadat ka janam-sthaan hai

Hallucination: pretraining rewards plausible continuation, and later stages often reward answering over abstaining. Sycophancy and verbosity: preference training, because raters like agreement and length. Reward hacking: RL against automatic checkers. Over-refusal: safety training that generalised too broadly. Model makers work against all of these, but no model is free of them.

Hallucination: pretraining vishwasniya lagne wale text ko inaam deta hai, aur baad ke stages aksar chup rehne se zyaada jawab dene ko inaam dete hain. Chaaploosi aur lambaai: preference training, kyunki raters ko haan aur lambaai pasand hai. Reward hacking: automatic checkers ke against RL. Zyaada mana karna: safety training jo zaroorat se zyaada fail gayi. Model banane wale in sab ke khilaaf kaam karte hain, par koi model inse poori tarah mukt nahi.

Harness note

You will rarely train; you will always steerTrain kabhi-kabhi karoge; steer hamesha karoge

Most companies never pre-train a model and rarely fine-tune one. Your levers are the harness: the prompt, the context you provide, the tools, the checks, and which model you route to. Fine-tuning is worth it for a narrow, high-volume task with lots of good examples, such as classifying tickets in your own categories. Start with prompting plus evals; fine-tune only when the evals show prompting has hit a ceiling.

Zyaadatar companies kabhi model pre-train nahi kartin aur fine-tune bhi kam karti hain. Aapke levers harness mein hain: prompt, diya gaya context, tools, checks, aur kis model par bhejna hai. Fine-tuning tab faayde ki hai jab kaam sankara ho, volume bada ho aur achhe examples bahut hon, jaise apni categories mein tickets classify karna. Prompting aur evals se shuru karo; fine-tune tabhi karo jab evals dikhaayein ki prompting ki had aa gayi.

Deep dive: the loss functions behind each schoolGehraai mein: har school ke peeche ke loss functionsadvanced
Pretraining / SFT: L = − Σt log πθ(xt | x<t) (4.1)
Bradley–Terry: P(A ≻ B) = σ( r(A) − r(B) ) (4.2)
RLHF: maxπ Ey∼π[ r(x, y) ] − β · KL( π ‖ πref ) (4.3)
DPO: L = − log σ( β [ log π(yw)/πref(yw) − log π(yl)/πref(yl) ] ) (4.4)

SFT uses the same loss as pretraining, but only on curated assistant replies. RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) learns a reward model with (4.2), then optimises (4.3), usually with PPO; β is the leash. DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) skips the separate reward model and optimises the preference pairs directly with (4.4). Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) replaces many human labels with AI feedback guided by written principles. RLVR trains on tasks with automatic checkers (maths answers, unit tests); GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) scores a group of sampled answers and pushes up the ones above the group average. This is how today's "reasoning" models learn long chains of thought.

SFT pretraining wala hi loss use karta hai, par sirf chune hue assistant jawabon par. RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) pehle (4.2) se reward model seekhta hai, phir (4.3) optimise karta hai, aksar PPO se; β hi patta hai. DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) alag reward model chhodkar (4.4) se preference pairs ko seedha optimise karta hai. Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) kai insaani labels ki jagah likhe hue siddhanton par chalne wala AI feedback use karta hai. RLVR aise kaamon par train karta hai jinke automatic checker hain (maths ke jawab, unit tests); GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) kai sampled jawabon ke group ko score karke group average se upar wale jawabon ko badhaata hai. Aaj ke "reasoning" models lambi soch isi tarah seekhte hain.

What we learnedHumne kya seekha

  1. I read the internet, then learned manners, then learned from report cards, then practised exams.
  2. Each school gave me a skill and a habit.
  3. If the judge has a bias, I learn the bias.
  4. Pushing a score too hard makes the score lie. Keep me on a leash, and check me with real tests.
  1. Maine internet padha, phir tameez seekhi, phir report cards se seekha, phir exams ki practice ki.
  2. Har school ne mujhe ek hunar aur ek aadat di.
  3. Agar judge mein bias hai, toh main woh bias seekh leta hoon.
  4. Score ke peeche zyaada bhaagne se score jhooth bolne lagta hai. Mujhe patte par rakho, aur asli tests se check karo.
